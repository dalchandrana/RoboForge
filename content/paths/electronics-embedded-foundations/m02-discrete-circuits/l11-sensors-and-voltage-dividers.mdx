## 1. Hook: How Does a Robot 'Feel'?
How does your eye sense daylight, or a thermostat detect the cold? They use materials whose physical properties shift when exposed to external energy.

In electronics, **sensors** act as sensory organs. Many fundamental sensors work by changing their electrical resistance!

## 2. Resistive Sensors
Two common examples found in robotics kits:
- **Light Dependent Resistor (LDR / Photoresistor):** Made of cadmium sulfide (CdS). In pitch darkness, resistance exceeds $100\text{ k}\Omega$. Under bright light, photons free electrons, dropping resistance to under $1\text{ k}\Omega$.
- **Thermistor (NTC):** A negative temperature coefficient resistor. As ambient temperature rises, thermal agitation frees carriers and resistance drops.

## 3. The Core Challenge: Measuring Resistance
A microcontroller cannot directly measure resistance. Its analog input pins only measure **voltage** ($0\text{ V}$ to $5\text{ V}$).

How do we turn a changing resistance into a changing voltage? With a **Voltage Divider**!

```
         +5V (Vcc)
           |
          [R1] (e.g. 10k Fixed Resistor)
           |
           +-----> V_out (To Microcontroller Analog Pin)
           |
          [R2] (e.g. LDR Sensor to Ground)
           |
          GND (0V)
```

## 4. The Voltage Divider Equation
When two resistors are placed in series across a voltage source $V_{\text{in}}$, the current through both is:
$$I = \frac{V_{\text{in}}}{R_1 + R_2}$$

The voltage dropped across the bottom resistor $R_2$ is:
$$V_{\text{out}} = I \times R_2 = V_{\text{in}} \times \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$$

### Worked Example:
Assume $V_{\text{in}} = 5\text{ V}$, fixed resistor $R_1 = 10\text{ k}\Omega$, and an LDR for $R_2$:
1. **In Bright Sunlight ($R_2 = 1\text{ k}\Omega$):**
   $$V_{\text{out}} = 5\text{ V} \times \frac{1\text{ k}\Omega}{10\text{ k}\Omega + 1\text{ k}\Omega} = 5 \times \frac{1}{11} \approx 0.45\text{ V}$$
2. **In Dark Shadow ($R_2 = 90\text{ k}\Omega$):**
   $$V_{\text{out}} = 5\text{ V} \times \frac{90\text{ k}\Omega}{10\text{ k}\Omega + 90\text{ k}\Omega} = 5 \times \frac{90}{100} = 4.5\text{ V}$$

By monitoring $V_{\text{out}}$, our robot instantly knows whether it is in shadow or bright sunlight!

## 5. Summary & Key Takeaways
- Sensors convert environmental stimuli into electrical signals.
- Resistive sensors need a series partner to form a voltage divider.
- Choosing a fixed resistor close to the sensor's mid-range resistance maximizes measurement sensitivity.
