## 1. Hook
Have you ever felt a tiny shock when touching a doorknob in winter?
That sudden little snap is static electricity jumping through the air.
Electricity powers everything from a wristwatch to an autonomous Mars rover!

<Callout type="safety" title="Safe Lab Practice">
In RoboForge, every hands-on circuit uses low-voltage DC batteries (under 12 volts). Never experiment with household wall sockets or mains power. Wall outlets carry deadly high voltages!
</Callout>

## 2. The Core Idea: What is Charge?
All matter around you is made of tiny atoms.
Atoms contain positive protons and negative electrons.
When electrons are pushed along a conductor like a copper wire, they form an **electric current**.

Think of electric current as water flowing through a closed garden hose.
- **Water particles** are like electric charges.
- **The water pump** acts like a battery pushing the charges.
- **The hose** is the conductive copper wire.

<Callout type="info" title="Where the Water Analogy Breaks">
Water can spill out into the open air from a cut hose. Electric current cannot spill onto your table! Charges will only flow if there is a complete, unbroken closed loop back to the battery.
</Callout>

## 3. See It: A Closed Circuit
To light up a small bulb or an LED, charges must travel in a complete circuit:

1. Electrons start at the negative terminal of the battery.
2. They travel through the connecting wire.
3. They pass through the light bulb, making it glow.
4. They return to the positive terminal of the battery.

If the wire is disconnected at any point, the flow stops instantly.

## 4. Try It Out
Imagine you have a battery, two wires, and an LED bulb.
What happens if you disconnect one wire?
Make your prediction:
- Will the light stay on for a second?
- Or will it turn off immediately?

<Reveal summary="See the explanation">
It turns off immediately! Electric current cannot jump across open air gaps at low voltages. The loop must remain closed.
</Reveal>
