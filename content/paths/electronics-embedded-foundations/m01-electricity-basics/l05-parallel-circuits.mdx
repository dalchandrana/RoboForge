## 1. Hook: Independent Branches
You switch off your bedroom lamp. Why does the fan keep spinning?

In homes and robots, components connect in **parallel**. Each device gets its own independent branch.

## 2. Voltage Across Parallel Branches
In parallel circuits, both ends of every part connect to common rails. 

Every parallel branch receives the exact same battery voltage:
$$V_{\text{total}} = V_1 = V_2 = V_3$$

A 9V battery provides 9V to every parallel branch.

## 3. Current Splits at Forks
Charges flow to a junction. The current splits into separate branches. 

Total current equals the sum of branch currents:
$$I_{\text{total}} = I_1 + I_2 + \dots + I_n$$

Low resistance branches carry more current. High resistance branches carry less.

<Callout type="safety" title="Current Accumulation Hazard">
Parallel branches combine their currents at the battery. Too many branches draw high current. This can overheat small battery cells. Keep total current below 2 Amperes.
</Callout>

## 4. Equivalent Parallel Resistance
Adding parallel branches creates new paths for charges. This lowers overall resistance:
$$\frac{1}{R_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2}$$

For two resistors, use product-over-sum:
$$R_{\text{eq}} = \frac{R_1 \times R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$$

Two equal 1000Ω resistors in parallel yield 500Ω. Parallel paths make current flow easier!
