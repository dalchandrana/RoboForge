## 1. Hook: Why Do We Need Obstacles?
If electric current does useful work (like lighting bulbs and spinning motors), why would we deliberately insert a component whose sole job is to **restrict** current?

Without resistance, an unlimited rush of electrons would surge through delicate semiconductors, overheating battery cells and instantly destroying LEDs! Resistors are the traffic controllers of physical electronics.

## 2. Resistance: Opposing the Flow
Resistance (symbol $R$) measures how strongly a material opposes the flow of electric charges. It is measured in **Ohms** (symbol $\Omega$).
- A thick copper wire has very low resistance (almost $0\,\Omega$).
- A carbon or metal-film resistor has precise resistance (e.g. $220\,\Omega$, $1\,\text{k}\Omega$, $10\,\text{k}\Omega$).
- Air and plastic have immense resistance (billions of Ohms), acting as insulators.

## 3. Ohm's Law: The Fundamental Equation
In 1827, German physicist Georg Ohm discovered that current is directly proportional to voltage and inversely proportional to resistance:
$$V = I \times R$$

From this single relationship, we can derive any missing parameter:
- **Find Voltage:** $V = I \times R$
- **Find Current:** $I = \frac{V}{R}$
- **Find Resistance:** $R = \frac{V}{I}$

### Worked Example:
Suppose a $9\text{ V}$ battery powers a circuit with a $300\,\Omega$ resistor. How much current flows?
$$I = \frac{9\text{ V}}{300\,\Omega} = 0.03\text{ A} = 30\text{ mA}$$

<Callout type="safety" title="Component Overheating Rule">
When current flows through a resistor, electrical power is dissipated as heat:
$$P = V \times I$$
Standard small resistors in our robotics kits are rated for $0.25\text{ W}$ (quarter-watt). If you put too much current through a tiny resistor, it will become hot to the touch. Always calculate power before wiring!
</Callout>

## 4. Reading Resistor Color Bands
Most through-hole resistors have 4 colored rings printed around their ceramic body:
1. **Band 1:** First significant digit (e.g., Brown = 1)
2. **Band 2:** Second significant digit (e.g., Black = 0)
3. **Band 3:** Multiplier decade (e.g., Red = $10^2 = 100$)
4. **Band 4:** Tolerance (Gold = $\pm 5\%$)

For example: Brown - Black - Red - Gold represents:
$$10 \times 100 = 1000\,\Omega = 1\,\text{k}\Omega$$
