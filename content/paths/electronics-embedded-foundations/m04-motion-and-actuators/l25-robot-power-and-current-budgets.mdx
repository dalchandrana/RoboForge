## 1. Hook: The Robot That Freezes on Takeoff
You finish assembling your two-wheel autonomous robot car. It sits on your desk, blinking its LEDs happily.

You turn on the drive motors. The wheels twitch forward for a fraction of a second, the robot dies, and the Arduino boots up from the beginning again! Why does the robot reboot every single time the motors start?

You have encountered **Voltage Sag and Brownout**!

<Callout type="safety" title="Battery & Power Safety">
  School-path robotics strictly uses safe low-voltage battery packs (4xAA alkaline or NiMH cells producing 4.8V to 6V DC, ≤ 2A). Never attempt to charge bare Lithium-Polymer (LiPo) or Lithium-Ion pouch cells without specialized balance chargers and dedicated fire-safe enclosures. Disconnect battery packs when leaving the laboratory bench.
</Callout>

## 2. Battery Internal Resistance ($R_{\text{int}}$) & Voltage Sag
A real battery is not an ideal infinite voltage source. It behaves like an ideal voltage source in series with an internal resistance ($R_{\text{int}}$):

$$V_{\text{terminal}} = V_{\text{battery}} - (I_{\text{load}} \times R_{\text{int}})$$

- In resting mode, current is tiny ($50\text{ mA}$): voltage drop is negligible.
- When two TT gearmotors suddenly accelerate, their stall surge current pulls $1.5\text{ A}$!
- The $1.5\text{ A}$ current through $R_{\text{int}}$ causes the battery terminal voltage to plummet from $6.0\text{ V}$ down to $3.5\text{ V}$!
- The Arduino's on-chip Brown-Out Detection (BOD) circuit detects the voltage drop and forces an immediate CPU hardware reset!

## 3. The Power Budget
To design a reliable robot, calculate your maximum and average current requirements:

| Subsystem | Idle Current | Peak / Operating Current |
|---|---|---|
| Arduino Uno (ATmega328P) | $45\text{ mA}$ | $50\text{ mA}$ |
| Ultrasonic Sensor (HC-SR04) | $2\text{ mA}$ | $15\text{ mA}$ |
| Line Tracking IR Modules (x2) | $20\text{ mA}$ | $40\text{ mA}$ |
| Two TT DC Gearmotors | $0\text{ mA}$ | $400\text{ mA}$ (running) / $1400\text{ mA}$ (stall) |
| SG90 Pan Servo | $10\text{ mA}$ | $500\text{ mA}$ (moving) |
| **Total Current Demand** | **$77\text{ mA}$** | **$2005\text{ mA}$ (Peak Peak)** |

Your power supply must be capable of delivering this peak surge current without dropping below the operating voltage threshold.

## 4. The Golden Rule of Robotics: Common Ground
To isolate heavy motor surges from delicate logic chips, roboticists often use **two power sources** (e.g. a USB power bank for the Arduino and a 4xAA battery pack for the motor driver).

When using separate supplies, you **MUST connect their Ground wires together**:
```
  [ Battery 1 (Motors) ]              [ Battery 2 (Logic) ]
       (+)      (-)                        (+)      (-)
        |        |                          |        |
        |        +--------------------------+--------+
        |                    |                       |
   Motors +6V        SHARED COMMON GROUND        Arduino GND (0V)
```
Without a shared common ground, control signals have no reference voltage and float erratically!

## 5. Summary
- Always calculate a full subsystem current budget.
- Account for motor stall surges and battery voltage sag.
- Always bond all power supply negative leads into a shared common ground.
