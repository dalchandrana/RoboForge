## 1. Hook: From Sparks to Motion
So far, our circuits have only moved electrons, blinked lights, and computed numbers.

Robotics is about making physical things move in the physical world! The workhorse of robotics motion is the **direct-current (DC) electric motor**.

<Callout type="safety" title="Motor Drive Safety">
  Motors are inductive loads that draw high current. Never power a DC motor directly from an Arduino I/O pin (pins are rated for 20mA max; motors draw 200mA to 800mA). Always use a dedicated battery pack or external supply limited to ≤ 12V DC and ≤ 2A. Never leave a stalled motor powered, as coils can overheat and cause burns.
</Callout>

## 2. How Brushed DC Motors Work
Inside a brushed DC motor:
1. **Permanent Magnets (Stator):** Create a stationary magnetic field.
2. **Coils of Copper Wire (Rotor / Armature):** When electric current passes through the wire, it creates an electromagnet.
3. **Lorentz Force:** Opposite magnetic poles attract and like poles repel, forcing the shaft to rotate.
4. **Commutator & Brushes:** As the shaft turns, mechanical brushes slide across copper contacts, reversing current direction every half-turn to keep rotation continuous.

## 3. Free-Running vs Stall Current
A DC motor has two critical operational states:
- **No-Load Current ($I_{\text{nl}}$):** Motor spins freely in the air without resistance (e.g., $100\text{ mA}$).
- **Stall Current ($I_{\text{stall}}$):** When wheels are locked in place by a heavy load or wall, rotation stops. The motor acts like a dead short circuit of bare copper wire! Current can surge to $800\text{ mA}$ or more, generating intense heat.

## 4. The Inductive Spike Hazard
Motor coils store energy in magnetic fields:
$$E = \frac{1}{2} L \cdot I^2$$

When you turn off a switching transistor, current drops to zero instantly ($dI/dt$ is huge). According to Faraday's law of induction ($V = -L \cdot dI/dt$), the collapsing magnetic field releases its stored energy as a massive high-voltage spike (often exceeding $50\text{ V}$ to $100\text{ V}$)!

This spike instantly punches through the thin silicon junction of your transistor or Arduino chip, destroying it permanently.

## 5. The Solution: The Flyback Diode
Place a rectifier diode (like a `1N4007`) in parallel with the motor terminals, with its cathode pointing toward the positive supply voltage:

```
        +5V / +6V (V_motor)
           |
       +---+---+
       |       |
      [M]     [D] (Flyback Diode: Cathode up)
       |       |
       +---+---+
           |
         [ NPN Transistor or MOSFET Switch ]
           |
          GND
```

When the transistor turns off, the inductive spike circulates harmlessly through the diode loop until its energy dissipates as heat, saving your circuit!

## 6. Summary
- Never drive motors directly from microcontroller pins.
- Budget for stall current, not just running current.
- Always install a flyback diode across inductive motor coils.
